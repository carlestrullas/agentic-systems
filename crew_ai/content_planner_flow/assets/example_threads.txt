# Thread 1

## Tweet 1
Train/test split, explained in 2 minutes:

## Tweet 2
Every model needs to be judged on data it has never seen.

That's the whole point of a test set.

But how you split matters more than most people think 👇

## Tweet 3
A random split works when every row is independent.

- shuffle the rows
- keep ~80% for training
- keep ~20% for testing

Simple. And often wrong.

## Tweet 4
If your data has time in it, a random split lets the model peek into the future.

Fix: train on the past, test on the most recent period.

## Tweet 5
If several rows belong to the same user, patient or product, they must stay together.

Otherwise the model memorises the entity instead of learning the pattern.

Fix: group-based splits.

## Tweet 6
TL;DR
- independent rows → random split
- time series → split by time
- grouped data → split by group

Get the split right and your metrics finally mean something.

# Thread 2

## Tweet 1
Precision vs. recall, without the jargon:

## Tweet 2
Precision: of everything the model flagged, how much was right?

Recall: of everything that should have been flagged, how much did the model catch?

## Tweet 3
Spam filter → you care about precision.
A real email in the spam folder is worse than one spam email in your inbox.

## Tweet 4
Fraud detection → you care about recall.
Missing a fraudulent payment costs much more than reviewing a false alarm.

## Tweet 5
There's no "best" metric.

There's only the metric that matches the cost of being wrong.

Pick it before you train, not after.
